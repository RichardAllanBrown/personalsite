At work, every decision that is expensive to reverse gets written down: the context, the options, what we picked and what it will cost us later. It is the single most useful habit I have picked up in fifteen years.

So, naturally, I tried it at home. The first record was about the kitchen tap. The second was about whether we needed a second record about the kitchen tap.

## What actually worked

Writing down *why* turned out to matter far more than writing down *what*. Six months later nobody remembers why the boiler is set to 19°C, but the log does.

The template is deliberately tiny. If it takes more than five minutes to fill in, nobody will do it, and that includes me.

```yaml
# HADR-NNN: <title>
status: proposed | accepted | regretted
context: what forced the decision
decision: what we chose
cost: what it makes harder later
```
